# Talk details

- **Title:** The Last Software Engineer
- **Speaker:** Kent C. Dodds
- **Context:** Where we are in terms of agentic driven software engineering
- **Mode:** Async

# Your summary

<!-- Gist of your understanding — the 2-3 sentence version, in your own words. -->

- Agentic tooling are only getting better and workflows are evolving fast.
- Agents can do most of the work which makes human judgement the primary factor for good direction and even success of projects/companies.
- Product engineering is the new role that is more aligned with modern software engineering and that we should all take a crack at making things to exercise and improve judgement.

# Key points & excerpts

- In recent years, the tools are doing more of the work that we traditionally have done which leaves us with less to do, but not really.
- We are experiencing a rapid growth in workflows or ways to make agentic tools more efficient and we honestly can't predict what else might come up. Thing come in and fall out of favour quick (i.e. prompt engineering, fine tuning, flow engineering and now loop engineering)
- Since we don't know where we are in terms of AI advancement or AI taking over our traditional work entirely, we need to be conscious of what is essential to leverage Ai (and in the future AGI). And the idea of "The last software engineer" tries to capture the scenario where, if most of the work is done purely by AI, there should still be a slice of work that is essential for success which only us as humans can satisfy. If there was only one last software engineer in the world, what can they provide to keep everything running. And the answer is, judgement.
- The term "Product Engineering" comes in. He claims that it is the more human side of software engineering which will be the pillar for future/modern software engineering.
- With AI, instead of being good at hitting target well, what is more emphasised now is **knowing** what targets to hit.
- A quote from his podcast guests: Building the thing right is downstream of building the right thing. - Wayne Allan
- Product engineer != product manager. PM are more involved with stakeholders/customers
